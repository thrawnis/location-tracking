Waypoints

A waypoint is any place you track — a restaurant, store, park, or anywhere else. Each waypoint has its own page with its address, map link, ratings, dishes, photos, and notes. You can add one manually or save one straight from the map's “Rate Me!” button.

Browse waypoints → Add a waypoint →

Waypoint ratings

Each person can leave one overall rating and review per waypoint. The waypoint's headline score is the average of everyone's ratings. When a Google listing is known, the app also shows Google's rating alongside yours for comparison. You can edit or delete your own review at any time from the waypoint's page.

Items & dishes

Within a waypoint you can list specific items or dishes (e.g. “Margherita pizza”) and rate each one individually. To rate a dish you first need to have rated the waypoint overall. Dish ratings are shown as a star and average next to each item.

Gluten-free info & votes

Waypoints can record their gluten-free accommodation (a dedicated GF kitchen, GF options, etc.) plus dietary notes. Anyone can cast an agree/disagree vote on whether the listed GF status is accurate, so the community keeps it honest.

Photos

Upload photos to a waypoint. Large images are resized automatically. Only the person who uploaded a photo (or a superuser/admin) can rotate or delete it.

Collections

A collection is your own curated group of waypoints — a trip, a theme, a wishlist (e.g. “Austin 2026” or “GF-safe spots”). Collections are private by default; mark one public to show it, read-only, on your profile. A superuser/admin can feature a public collection so every waypoint in it links back to it — handy for things like a company's rotating food-truck list.

Manage collections →

Groups

A group is a private circle of friends — not listed or searchable anywhere. You bring people in by sharing the group's invite link, and an existing member approves each request. A group's page shows the waypoints its members have rated, so you can see how your circle scored a place separately from the overall average.

Your groups →

Linked accounts

Linked accounts are one-to-one connections that let you log waypoint and dish reviews on each other's behalf. Share your personal invite link; when someone opens it and approves, the two of you are linked. Either of you can then add a review attributed to the other — but only the person a review belongs to (or an admin) can edit or delete it. Links are direct: being linked to two people never links them to each other.

Manage linked accounts →

Reviews added for you

When a linked account logs a review on your behalf, it shows up here (and a banner points you to it the next time you sign in). Every review is attributed to you, so you stay in full control — edit or delete any of them from the waypoint's page.

Reviews added for you →

Chain linking

Two waypoints that are branches of the same chain (e.g. two Burger King locations) can be linked so they share one pooled list of items/dishes and their reviews, while each branch keeps its own address, hours, and overall rating. Use the “This is a chain” button on a waypoint's page to search for and link another branch. Detaching a branch is a superuser/admin action.

Map & nearby search

Set a location (type a city/zip, or use your device's location) and search nearby for places by name or category. Results you don't have yet can be saved as waypoints and rated on the spot with “Rate Me!”. The list defaults to sorting by distance from your location.

Open the map & search →

Import & export

Import your saved places from Google Takeout (or any GeoJSON file) to bulk-add waypoints, and export all waypoints as CSV, GeoJSON, or KML for backup or use in Google My Maps, Google Earth, or a GPS app.

Activity feed

Your activity feed is a running log of your own recent adds, edits, and deletes across waypoints, reviews, items, and photos.

Account & security

Protect your account with two-factor authentication (a TOTP app like Google Authenticator or Authy). It's optional for regular members and required for admins.

Two-factor settings →

Roles: superuser & admin

Most people are ordinary members who manage their own content. A superuser can edit or delete anyone's content (waypoints, items, photos, reviews) and feature collections, but has no access to site infrastructure. An admin has every superuser ability plus the management dashboard, user management, audit log, and site settings.
